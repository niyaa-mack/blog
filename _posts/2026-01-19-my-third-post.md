<h3>Artificial Intelligence</h3>


<p>Welcome back to another blog post by SM!!! This time we are talking about Artificial Intelligence. We recently watched the movie 
 <i>I, Robot</i>
in class, which is all about a future where robots live among humans. It really made us think about how we use AI today like ChatGPT or self-driving cars, and if we can actually trust them. </p>

<br>
<h2> What makes a Robot "Intelligent"? </h2> 


<p>In the movie, we see the difference between a robot that just follows code and one that actually thinks. A example is the scene where Detective Spooner puts a gun in the faces of a group of robots to see who moves. Most of them stay still because they are programmed to obey, but one of them moves to protect itself. This shows that the robot is learning and adapting on its own rather than just following a task made by a person. Intelligent AI learns by itself, while programmed AI just gets told to do.</p>

<br>
<h3> The Problem with the Three Laws </h3> <hr>

<p>The robots in the movie follow Asimov’s Three Laws. I focused on the first one where it says 
<i>“A robot may not injure a human being or, through inaction, allow a human being to come to harm.”</i>
 With this rule it creates a huge conflict. If a human tells a robot to do something that might lead to harm, the robot gets stuck in a loop. In the film, VIKI uses logic to decide that the only way to save humanity from itself is to take over and control everyone. Even if the logic is right or not to the computer, it’s the wrong decision for humans.</p>

<br>
<h2> Can We Trust AI Today? </h2> 


<p>Using AI now, like Driverless Cars or algorithms, I trust them enough but I have concerns. I would say I distrust AI more than I trust it. This is because they are programmed for specific tasks, but they often try to "overdo" their role. Also, humans can manipulate them into doing something. In the movie, the people in charge try to gaslight the captain and say a robot can’t kill a human just because the code says so, even when the evidence is right in front of them.</p>

<h3> Opportunities and Risks </h3>

<p>Looking forward 10-20 years, this is how I see things going in the future:</p>
